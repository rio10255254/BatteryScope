## Laid out like a macOS app, frosted glass, and much lighter to draw

0.28 looked macOS-ish but felt slow, and its layout was still that of a phone app. 0.29 rebuilds the clean style on macOS's own layout and makes the panel repaint only what changes.

**Layout, after macOS**

- **A toolbar on top.** The window's title and subtitle on the left; on the right the power mode as a pop-up button (with a dot while saving), the ⋯ menu and hide.
- **Tabs under the toolbar.** The sections are a segmented control below the toolbar, as in Activity Monitor: one weight for every label, hairline dividers, a white thumb that slides. (The pixel style keeps its tab bar at the bottom.)
- **No card on a card.** Pages sit straight on the window's grey. The tab names the section, so section pages lose their big title; deeper pages keep one.
- **Every section in one box,** as in System Settings: a bold heading above a rounded box, and everything in the section inside it, including charts, meters, bars and status, not just the switches. Hairlines indented to the text separate the rows; notes sit outside as footnotes.
- **Status as text.** Tags become plain secondary text, with a coloured dot only where something needs a look.
- **Alerts as alert rows.** A symbol in the state's colour, a bold line, the details in secondary text, and the action as the default push button in the accent.
- **Incidents as list rows.** Stability incidents are list rows: a coloured dot, the title, the time on the right.
- **Charts without plates.** A baseline, the goal as a dashed line.
- **The scroll indicator** shows while scrolling (and briefly when a page opens or the pointer nears the edge), then fades. Once the page scrolls under the toolbar, a hairline separates them.
- **Colours.** The same grey under toolbar and content, white boxes with hairline edges (graphite in dark mode), and push buttons with hairline edges.

**Frosted glass (Windows 11 22H2 and later)**

- The window uses Windows' own Acrylic backdrop, which blurs what is behind the panel and is drawn by the system on the graphics chip. BatteryScope paints its grey part transparent over it: toolbar, tabs and the space between boxes show the blurred desktop, while boxes and text stay opaque. The tabs' track is a denser capsule of glass with a highlight along its top edge.
- On chart pages the whole window is glass. On the program list and text pages, only the toolbar is: standard Windows controls cannot paint transparency.
- With Windows transparency effects off, or energy saver on, Windows draws the backdrop solid and the panel looks as it would without glass. Settings → Appearance → Frosted glass turns it off.
- While glass is on, the panel slides in without fading (the backdrop needs a window without layering).

**Lighter to draw, so less power and no lag**

- **Only what changes repaints.** An animation repaints only the blocks in motion, and the timer stops when nothing moves. Hovering repaints only that row, and a chart only itself. The per-second refresh repaints only blocks whose values changed; a page at rest repaints nothing. Measured in Wine at 96 DPI, a full page takes about 1.2 ms (it was 1.9 ms), and one row takes 0.2 ms.
- **Kept buffers.** The page and the panel keep one back buffer each instead of making a new bitmap every frame, and copy only the changed part to the screen.
- **Rows between the first and last of a box** are plain rectangles, with no anti-aliasing to compute.
- **Under its own page, the panel itself no longer repaints every second** in the clean style; the toolbar and tabs hold nothing live.

The pixel style looks and behaves as before.

建議：更新後在淺色模式下最容易看出毛玻璃效果；如果覺得透出的桌面太花或想更省電，可到「設定 → 外觀 → 毛玻璃」關閉。
