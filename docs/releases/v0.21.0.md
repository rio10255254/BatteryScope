## 60 Hz on battery by itself, every saving switch on the Saving tab, and a smoother, more polished panel

**60 Hz on battery, full rate plugged in.** BatteryScope switches the screen to 60 Hz when you unplug and back to the panel's highest rate (120 Hz on the G14) when you plug in. It is on by default. In the last full test this was the largest single saving: 1.5 W less while browsing, and 0.7 W less on a still screen or video. It changes the rate once per switch of power source, so a rate you pick by hand afterwards stays. When dynamic saving also manages the rate, it decides on battery; during a measurement the lab decides.

**Saving switches.** The Saving tab now starts its controls with switches you turn on or off with one click. Each has a line saying what it does:
- dynamic saving;
- 60 Hz on battery;
- background efficiency mode;
- dark mode;
- automatic GPU Eco, keyboard backlight off when idle, and Slash lighting off on battery (ASUS);
- the ASUS performance mode and low-battery saving, which open their choices.

The settings page lists 60 Hz on battery too.

**Motion and detail:**
- Opening a page: its sections rise and fade in one after another, bars and meters grow to their values, and the power chart rises from left to right.
- Live values glide to their new numbers instead of jumping.
- Switches slide their knob, and rows light up under the pointer and give a little when pressed.
- The wheel and page keys scroll smoothly.
- The tab highlight slides to the tab you pick.
- Menus fade in and settle into place.
- Section headings carry a small accent mark.

**Notifications and notices** share one look in the panel's pixel or smooth style, and rise and fade in and out:
- **Panel notices:** the answer to something done in the panel shows as a card above the tabs for a few seconds, instead of a line of green text.
- **Alerts:** low battery, background drain, and updates appear as a card by the clock. A click opens the page they are about, and the card stays while the pointer is on it. While a full-screen game or a presentation is on, or in quiet hours, Windows' own notification is used instead.
- **Key display:** brightness, backlight, Fn lock and the rest use the same style.

All motion follows the Windows animation setting.
