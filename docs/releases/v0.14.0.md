## One-click automatic test, dynamic power saving, and a new settings page

**Automatic test (Power lab → Automatic test, about 1.5 hours).** Unplug, click once, and leave the PC on. Realistic activities run in a separate full-screen Edge (or Chrome) profile served by BatteryScope from 127.0.0.1: web browsing, 1080p video with sound, typing, a static page, and sustained compute. Power mode, refresh rate, CPU Boost, energy preference, background-app efficiency mode, brightness, and finally screen off are each switched back and forth in randomized pairs under the activities they affect. The test measures:
- battery, CPU core, and rest-of-system power;
- late frames and dropped video frames;
- page-load and keystroke response times;
- work rate and energy per unit of work.

When it ends it restores every setting, saves a system snapshot (power plans, battery report, hardware, processes, startup apps), and exports everything to the desktop with the summary copied. Esc stops it; `BatteryScope.exe --autotest` starts it.

**Dynamic power saving (experimental, off by default).** On battery, power mode, energy preference, and CPU Boost follow what you are doing. Settings save power while idle or reading, respond at once when you use the PC, and give performance under heavy load. It switches toward performance immediately and toward saving only after 6 seconds, with pacing between changes. It leaves a setting alone for 10 minutes when G-Helper or you change it. Settings the automatic test proves on this PC are used, and settings it shows to cost smoothness are vetoed. Optional: battery target, refresh rate switching, and efficiency mode for busy background apps. Everything is restored on AC, in games, when turned off, or after a crash.

**Settings redesigned.** The ⋯ menu is short again; every option lives on a new settings page grouped into power saving, alerts, appearance, and general, where each row shows its value and switches with one click.

**Menus in the app's own style.** The ⋯ menu, the tray menu, and the mini window menu no longer use the Windows popup menu. They use the panel's surface, fonts, and pixel or smooth style, with taller rows and an On/Off pill for options. Submenus open in place with a back row instead of flying out. Arrow keys, Enter, Esc, and Left/Backspace (back) all work.

Not yet verified on real hardware: the browser kiosk launch, brightness and screen-off experiments, and the governor's real savings. The automatic test is how they will be measured.
