## Works across Windows 10 and 11 laptops and desktops

BatteryScope 0.11.0 picks its CPU energy source from whatever the PC provides instead of requiring AMD's per-core counters:

- **Per-core** (AMD Ryzen): unchanged, the most precise.
- **All-core total** (most Intel PCs, multi-package workstations, and PCs with more than 64 logical processors): one energy pool split by CPU time or cycles, with a 0.8–1.25× range.
- **Package only**: package energy above a learned 10-minute idle floor is split; the top of the range also shares the floor.
- **None**: no watts are invented; apps are ranked by CPU/I/O activity as before.

The details page shows which source is in use, and `--diagnose` now lists the Windows build, processor groups, energy source, and every EMI channel name.

Desktops without a battery show CPU package power, core power, unattributed power, and a 30-minute CPU W chart on the main panel, and the On battery power mode is disabled. On Windows 10, the power mode page uses the Windows 10 power slider: both settings are shown, and the one for the current power source can be changed.

Validation: per-core mode remains verified on a Ryzen 9 8945HS. The new energy tiers are unit-tested; the Windows 10 power slider path, Intel all-core/package tiers, multi-package systems, and desktops have not yet been verified on real hardware.
